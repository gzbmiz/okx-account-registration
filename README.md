### How maker and taker fees work

A maker order adds liquidity to the order book, usually by placing a limit order that is not filled immediately. A taker order removes liquidity by matching with an existing order, which commonly occurs with a market order or an immediately executable limit order.

For spot trading, the general calculation is:

text
Trading fee = fee rate × filled trade amount


For example, if a fee rate is 0.350% and the filled amount is 1,000 units of the quoted trading value, the fee is calculated from the applicable filled amount. The exact currency deducted depends on the product and order direction. OKX advises users to check the fee shown on the order and in the trade history instead of assuming that every market uses the same rate.

The table is useful for understanding the structure, but it should not be treated as a permanent price list. OKX says fees may be reviewed by trading pair or fee group, and the rates displayed for a specific account and market are the ones that apply at the time of trading.
